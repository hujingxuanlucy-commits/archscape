Why a route, not a feed

Architects and building enthusiasts already stop constantly to notice a space — but most of those photos end there. No context on what the building is, no memory of the walk that led to it, no one to share it with.

A route turns that habit into something visible. It doesn't just log the building — it keeps the walk itself: the order things were seen in, the space moved through to get there. That's why the map is built around lines, not a grid of pins.

Once a route exists, it also becomes a way to find people. Someone nearby with an overlapping route is probably worth talking to — maybe even worth a short coffee break together.

What you can do
Point, or shoot first

Capture

Hold the camera up and it reads the frame on its own — or tap the shutter to shoot first and match after. Every match comes with a confidence score, so a wrong guess is one tap from a rescan.

Nearby, trending, or yours

Discover

Switch between what's around you, what's picking up attention, and what you've saved. The map surfaces "paths crossing" — the spots where other explorers' routes overlap with yours.

Toggle the lines

Journey Map

Every capture drops onto the map as part of a line. Layer Architecture, Art Spaces, and Photography together, and see the distance, the stops, and the time behind today's walk.

Find people through what they've seen

Community

Browse real check-ins, tagged by style and city, from explorers worldwide. Follow the ones whose eye you like, or join a group built around a shared interest.

Captures, saved, journeys

Profile

A running record of what you've shot, what you've kept for later, and the routes you've walked start to finish — the beginning of a personal image library.

The design system

Cormorant Garamond carries the voice of the app — editorial, a little formal. Inter handles everything you tap. Colour is reserved almost entirely for the three lines.
